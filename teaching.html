Teaching & service

Computational linguistics is undergoing huge disruptions. Courses that were taught the same way for decades now need major revamps, or are becoming a thing of the past. Like pretty much everyone else, I am trying to navigate the changes that AI is causing in the classroom.

Flagship courses

  • Language and Computation LIN 1775
  • Computational Linguistics LIN 5770
  • Corpus Linguistics LIN 5774 (being reworked)

Working with students

I enjoy working with students. I regularly supervise and mentor undergraduate research (UROP) and graduate research assistants, and I serve on doctoral committees. Students in my group have co-authored peer-reviewed papers on AI and language. If you are passionate about language and interested in language technology and programming (at any level), I would love to hear from you:

  • In your first or second year at FSU, the Undergraduate Research Opportunity Program (UROP) is a great way to work with me. If you are eligible and interested, please reach out.
  • In your third or fourth year, or in a master’s programme at FSU, you are welcome to ask about contributing to research, or about doing a PhD in Scientific Computing. Do reach out and tell me what you are curious about.
  • Not yet at FSU and considering a PhD with me? Please start with the PhD page.

Service

Service to the field matters to me. I review regularly for the Association for Computational Linguistics (ACL Rolling Review and the main conferences) and have served on review panels for the National Science Foundation. I am proud to have received an ACL Outstanding Reviewer Award (2026).
