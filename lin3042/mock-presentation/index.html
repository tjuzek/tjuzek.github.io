The article and its question

Do AI words rise in the news after ChatGPT, across languages?

In scientific English, words that AI models overuse, such as delve, underscore and intricate, have spiked since 2022 (e.g. Kobak et al. 2025). But that work is almost all English, and almost all science.

Why it is worth doing: news is what most people read, and most languages have not been looked at.

H0AI words change no more than comparable baseline words. H1AI words rise more than baseline words.

What the researchers did · 1: finding the AI words

No participants here: the ‘participants’ are texts, news sentences in 34 languages.

A news sentence from 2020–21, cut in halfThe new bridge over the river opened on Monday,|…

GPT-4.1-mini writes the second half… and the mayor said it would revitalize the waterfront and emphasize the city’s commitment to its residents.

The journalist’s real second half… two years late, after the cost had doubled.

Example made up for this slide. The study used up to 100,000 real sentences per language.

Words the model uses far more often than the journalists are its AI words: the top 20 per language, each language computed on its own, nothing translated. English top 10: additionally, emphasize, revolutionize, revitalize, captivated, conservationist, streamline, firsthand, personalize, introspective.

One piece of evidence: Figure 2

AI words against baseline words, in 34 languages

0One row per language. Open circle: baseline words. Filled mark: AI words. Further right means a bigger rise from 2020–21 to 2023–24.

1English: AI words +25.5%; the baseline words fall a little.

2The dashed lines are the averages: AI words +15.1%, baseline words −4.5%.

3Biggest rises: Romanian +88.9%, German +70.8%, Czech +49.7%.

4Not everywhere: in six languages (purple) AI words fall below their baseline, e.g. Persian −33.2%, Japanese −29.9%.

5In 28 of 34 languages, AI words outpace the baseline: evidence for H1. Real, but modest: smaller than in scientific English.

Critique

Strength

A built-in control

Baseline words are counted the same way, in the same texts. And 34 languages, each with its own list: nothing translated.

Weakness

Correlation, not causation

The counts cannot tell apart AI-written text, people picking up the words, and topics shifting.

With billions of tokens, every language passes the significance test, even the eight where AI words fell. The comparison with the baseline carries the claim.

Follow-up

Follow the outlets

Track the same news outlets into 2025–26, and compare outlets that say they use AI with outlets that say they do not. This needs a corpus that records the outlet; News Crawl does not.

Why this article Tommie: rewrite in your words

I had looked at why ChatGPT ‘delves’ so much in science writing (Juzek & Ward 2025). I wanted to know whether this is an English-and-science thing, or whether it reaches everyday news, in many languages.

What it changed for me: the effect is real, but smaller in news than in science, and not universal. Six languages go the other way.
